The warden sent for her before breakfast. He sat behind a table covered in maps, and he did not ask her to sit. A rider tells me, he said, that the Lady Isolde of Varrow spent last evening kneeling in the snow beside a dragon who has burned four men, sewing her wing. He tells me the dragon allowed it.

Maelle said yes, my lord. He asked where a lady of Varrow had learned to sew like that. She said her old nurse had taught her. It was the kind of lie that only works if nobody cares enough to check, and she watched him decide, for now, not to care. He looked out of the window at the yard, where Veyra lay with her head raised, watching the tower. If you can close that wing, he said at last, you may try. She is the oldest dragon on the pass. I would rather not put her down.

So Maelle went to work. Every afternoon, while the light lasted, she knelt beside the wing with Hallam at her side. He could not sew, but he knew the wing: where the bones ran under the membrane, where it would bear weight and where it would only pretend to. He held the edges in his one hand and she drew them together with the fine needles from her mother's case, a few inches a day, never more than the dragon could stand. The riders stopped laughing after the first week. By the second, some of them stopped by with hot wine to watch.

On the eighteenth day Veyra lowered her wing for her. She simply laid it down flat across the stones, the long tear turned up toward Maelle's hands, and closed her eyes. That was the day they began the long seam, from shoulder to tip.

On the twentieth day a courier was caught on the pass. When the riders searched him they found a sealed letter under the lining of his saddle. It was addressed to Lord Castellan, whose lands lay on the far side of the mountains. The seal was the grey hawk of House Varrow.

The warden sent for Maelle again that evening. This time he told her to sit. He read the letter to her himself, slowly, and she recognised every turn of phrase, because she had heard that voice through the sewing room door for eight years. The renewal will not be signed. The pass will be yours by the first thaw, as agreed, and my daughter will be your son's wife before midsummer. You need not concern yourself with the girl at Cold Reach. She is not my daughter. She is of no consequence to anyone.

The warden folded the letter. He said, The law of the pass says the hostage of a broken treaty answers for it at the gate. It does not say what happens to a hostage who is not who she claims to be. So I will ask you once, and you will think carefully before you answer. Who are you?

Maelle looked at her hands. She thought of her father's grave, and of Odrenne in the sewing room doorway, and of how easy it would be to keep being Isolde for a few more days. She said, I am the woman who is mending your dragon, my lord. May I finish before I answer you?

The warden looked at her for a long time. Then he nodded. The renewal is in six days, he said. Castellan's riders are already gathering on the far side of the pass. Finish quickly.

She worked by lantern light after that. On the fifth night, with her fingers bleeding and Hallam asleep against the wall, she tied the last knot at the tip of the long seam and cut the thread. Veyra opened her eyes. Slowly, carefully, the old dragon lifted the mended wing and spread it wide against the stars, and held it there, trembling, and did not fly.
