The renewal was held at the Cold Reach gate, as it always had been, on a clear, cold morning. The warden waited at a stone table set in the gateway itself, with the treaty scroll unrolled before him. And up the long road from the south, in a black carriage with the grey hawk painted on its door, came Odrenne of Varrow, to renew the treaty in person.

She stepped down in grey silk and furs. She greeted the warden with perfect courtesy. Then she turned, as custom required, to greet the hostage, and for the first time in twenty-six days she looked Maelle full in the face. Nothing moved in Odrenne's expression. Only her eyes went, very briefly, to Maelle's hands, and then to the yard behind her, where an old dragon lay watching with her wing folded against her side.

The warden asked House Varrow to set its seal to the renewal. Odrenne smiled, and said that House Varrow had considered the matter carefully, and would not. And as the words left her mouth, a horn sounded from the far side of the pass.

They came over the eastern ridge in a long dark line: Castellan's riders, twenty dragons or more, flying low and fast toward the gate. The riders of Cold Reach ran for their mounts. The warden stood, and his face was grey, and he said the words the law required of him. The treaty is broken. The hostage will answer for it at the gate. Two riders took Maelle by the arms and walked her to the centre of the gateway, into the open, where every dragon in the sky could see her. Odrenne stepped back toward her carriage and did not look at her again.

Maelle stood in the cold wind and watched the dark line come on. She was not thinking about Odrenne, or Isolde, or the law of the pass. She was thinking about the long seam, and whether the knots at the tip would hold.

Behind her, in the yard, something enormous moved.

Veyra rose onto her feet. She spread both wings, the whole one and the mended one, and the gold light of the morning poured through the new seam like light through a church window. She beat them once, and the snow of the yard lifted in a white wave. She beat them again, and she was in the air. She flew low over the gate, so low that her shadow passed over Maelle's face, and she went straight at the dark line of Castellan's riders, alone, roaring, with fire in her throat.

The first of the enemy dragons broke away. Then the next. Castellan's riders had been told the old dragon of Cold Reach would never fly again. Within minutes the dark line was scattering back over the eastern ridge, and Veyra was circling slowly above the gate, over the one person she had chosen to defend.

The warden walked out into the gateway. He took Odrenne's letter from his coat, and in front of both houses, and every rider on the wall, he read it aloud, all the way to the last line. She is not my daughter. She is of no consequence to anyone.

Then he turned to Maelle. I asked you once, he said. I am asking you again, here, where everyone can hear. By the law of the pass, a hostage who is not who she claims to be has no protection under the treaty. Think carefully. Who are you?

Maelle looked at Odrenne. She thought of her father's grave, and of the sewing room door, and of eight years of being no one. Then she looked up at the dragon circling in the clear sky, on a wing she had closed stitch by stitch with her own hands.

My name is Maelle, she said. I am the daughter of Aldren of Varrow. I have never been Isolde. And I am not of no consequence.
